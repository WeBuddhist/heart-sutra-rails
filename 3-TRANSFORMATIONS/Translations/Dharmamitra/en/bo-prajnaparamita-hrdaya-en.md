# The Heart of the Blessed Mother, the Perfection of Wisdom ^0

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^0-1]]

The Heart of the Blessed Mother, the Perfection of Wisdom. ^0-1

## The Introductory Narrative of the Sutra ^1-0

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^1-1]]

In the language of India: Bhagavatīprajñāpāramitāhṛdaya. ^1-1

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^1-2]]

In the language of Tibet: The Heart of the Blessed Mother, the Perfection of Wisdom. ^1-2

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^1-3]]

Fascicle one. ^1-3

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^1-4]]

Homage to the Bhagavatī Prajñāpāramitā. ^1-4

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^1-5]]

Thus have I heard at one time: ^1-5

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^1-6]]

The Blessed One was residing at Vulture Peak Mountain in Rājagṛha, together with a great community of monks and a great community of bodhisattvas. ^1-6

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^1-7]]

At that time, the Blessed One entered into the meditative absorption on the categories of phenomena called "Profound Illumination." ^1-7

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^1-8]]

Also at that time, the bodhisattva mahāsattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara, was contemplating the very practice of the profound perfection of wisdom. ^1-8

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^1-9]]

He also beheld those five aggregates as being empty of intrinsic nature. ^1-9

## The Actual Meaning of the Sūtra ^2-0

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-1]]

Then, through the power of the Buddha, the venerable Śāriputra spoke these words to the bodhisattva mahāsattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara: ^2-1

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-2]]

"How should any son of noble family who wishes to engage in the practice of the profound perfection of wisdom train?" When he had spoken thus, the bodhisattva mahāsattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara, said to the venerable Śāradvatīputra: "Śāriputra, any son of noble family or daughter of noble family who wishes to engage in the practice of the profound perfection of wisdom should behold it in this way: they should correctly and repeatedly behold those five aggregates also as being empty of intrinsic nature." ^2-2

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-3]]

Form is emptiness; emptiness is form. ^2-3

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-4]]

Emptiness is not other than form; form is also not other than emptiness. ^2-4

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-5]]

Likewise, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness are empty. ^2-5

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-6]]

Śāriputra, therefore, all phenomena are emptiness; they are without characteristic, unborn, unceasing, stainless, not free from stain, not deficient, and not complete. ^2-6

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-7]]

Śāriputra, therefore, in emptiness there is no form, no feeling, no perception, no mental formations, and no consciousness. There is no eye, no ear, no nose, no tongue, no body, and no mind. There is no form, no sound, no smell, no taste, no touch, and no mental phenomena. ^2-7

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-8]]

There is no eye element, and so on, up to no mind element, and even no mental consciousness element. ^2-8

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-9]]

There is no ignorance, and no exhaustion of ignorance, and so on, up to no aging and death, and no exhaustion of aging and death. There is no suffering, no origin, no cessation, and no path. There is no wisdom, no attainment, and also no non-attainment. ^2-9

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-10]]

Śāriputra, therefore, because bodhisattvas have no attainment, they rely on and dwell in the perfection of wisdom. ^2-10

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-11]]

Since there is no obscuration in their minds, they have no fear; having utterly transcended all error, they reach the ultimate culmination of nirvāṇa. ^2-11

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-12]]

All the buddhas who reside throughout the three times also manifestly and completely awaken to unsurpassed, perfect, and complete enlightenment by relying on the perfection of wisdom. ^2-12

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-13]]

Therefore, the mantra of the perfection of wisdom, the mantra of great insight, the unsurpassed mantra, the mantra equal to the unequaled, the mantra that completely pacifies all suffering, should be known as true because it is not false. The mantra of the perfection of wisdom is proclaimed: ^2-13

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-14]]

tadyathā oṃ gate gate pāragate pārasaṃgate bodhi svāhā ^2-14

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-15]]

Śāriputra, a bodhisattva mahāsattva should train in the profound perfection of wisdom in this way. ^2-15

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-16]]

Then the Blessed One arose from that samādhi and gave his approval to the bodhisattva mahāsattva, the noble Avalokiteśvara, saying, “Excellent! Excellent! Son of noble family, it is just so. ^2-16

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-17]]

Son of noble family, it is just so; one should practice the profound perfection of wisdom in the very way that you have taught. ^2-17

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-18]]

The Tathāgatas, too, will rejoice.” ^2-18

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-19]]

When the Blessed One had spoken these words, ^2-19

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-20]]

the venerable Śāriputra, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Avalokiteśvara, that entire assembly, and the world with its gods, humans, asuras, and gandharvas rejoiced and highly praised what the Blessed One had said. ^2-20

![[bo-prajnaparamita-hrdaya#^2-21]]

The Noble Heart of the Perfection of Wisdom is complete. ^2-21
